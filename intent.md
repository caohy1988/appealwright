# Intent

Appealwright is a B2B workstation for property-tax appeal firms. The buyer is a firm principal who runs a desk of analysts. The user is the analyst who has to turn an address and an assessed value into a board-ready appeal packet, dozens of times a week, during a short filing window.

## Why this exists

Firms win appeals on comps and lose them on time. Today an analyst opens the assessor portal, pulls a handful of nearby sales, adjusts them by hand in a spreadsheet, eyeballs a trend, then writes the same letter for the fortieth time. The research is repetitive; the letter is formulaic; the judgment is not. We automate the first two and leave the third to the analyst.

## What we are pitching

- One click from subject to draft: address plus assessed value in, comps table plus 12-month trend plus grounds plus a formal Board of Equalization letter out, with every step visible in an agent timeline.
- The letter is grounded, not generated from thin air. Every number in it traces to a comp row the analyst can see and edit.
- Firm-shaped pricing. Seats, not per-appeal fees. A firm buys a desk, not a transaction.

## What we are not pitching

- Not a consumer product. No homeowner self-service flow.
- Not a filing service. Output is a draft for human review, not legal advice, not a filing.
- Not a live MLS or assessor integration. The prototype runs over a committed, labelled synthetic comp set for King County, WA.
- Not a clone of any existing appeal firm's brand, copy, colors, or contingency-fee model.

## Demo tenant

Northshore Appeals, a fictional firm in Kirkland, WA working King County residential appeals. The demo org is on the Team plan with 4 of 10 seats filled.

## Success for this prototype

A reviewer can open the pitch page, click through to the workstation, draft an appeal on a seeded over-assessed case, watch the agent work, read a letter that would not embarrass a real analyst, print it, and understand in under three minutes why a firm would pay per seat for this.
